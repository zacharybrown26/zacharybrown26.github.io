How I got here

Three Interests, One Direction

I did not get to finance through the stock market. I got here through three things that had nothing to do with money and everything to do with how downside actually behaves: it is quiet until it isn’t, and it is always someone’s job to be ready for it.

I am a Finance and Accounting student at Florida State carrying a 3.9, and I have spent the last two years working one thesis from three different seats. Fixed income at Aevum Capital, municipal credit at United Capital, lower-middle-market deal sourcing at Waterbrook Advisors, and equity research for FSU’s $11.5M Student Investment Fund. I am recruiting for investment banking now, as the training ground for the buy-side.

01

Goalkeeping

Draft — your story goes here

Two or three sentences, in your voice, to open this section: when you went in goal, your UPSL club, the season you made Miami Herald All-Conference Second Team, and one goal you conceded that you still think about. The analysis below only works if a real match sits in front of it.

A striker can miss ten chances and still be carried off the field for the eleventh. A keeper can be perfect for eighty-nine minutes and lose the match in the ninetieth. It is the only position on the pitch where the scoring is openly asymmetric: you are not credited for the saves, you are debited for the goals, and the job is to make the difference between those two numbers as boring as possible.

That is also the exact shape of a credit investment, which is why the fixed income work made immediate sense to me. Lend well and you get par and a coupon. Lend badly and you get the whole loss. At Aevum I built models measuring how far prices move on a 100bp shift and compared investment grade against high yield spreads over Treasuries, looking for where investors were genuinely paid for the risk. At United Capital I checked whether an issuer’s project cash flows actually covered debt service. Both of those are goalkeeping questions. Not how good can this get, but what beats me, and from where.

The other thing about the position is the sightline. You are the only player who sees the entire field at once, and organizing the eleven in front of you matters more than anything you do with your hands.

I want a seat where the downside is mine to hold for five years, not one where the work ends when the deal closes.

02

Jiu-Jitsu

Draft — your story goes here

When you started, where you train, your belt, and one specific roll you lost that changed how you train. No photo needed, this section reads fine without one.

Jiu-jitsu is the only place I have been taught leverage as a physical fact rather than a line in a model. A smaller person wins from position, and the identical grip that finishes a match from on top gets you finished from underneath. Nothing about the leverage changes. Only the position does.

When my team modeled Project Alpha we held entry and exit at the same 7.5x, which meant every dollar of return had to come from operations and paying down debt rather than from the multiple moving in our favor. Debt did not make that business good. It made our position on it more permanent, in whichever direction it already pointed.

Underwriting leverage honestly is the part of this job I actually want, not the part I tolerate to get to the returns.

Living Somewhere Else

Draft — one thing I need

I have the Sahara and I have London. What I do not have is what either place actually changed for you. The paragraphs below are my argument, not your observation, and a reader can tell the difference. One or two sentences of what you genuinely noticed will do it.

Also send the full-resolution original of the Morocco photo. The copy I have is 360×480 and it is capped small on the page so it does not look soft.

I grew up in Boca Raton, which is about as far from the Sahara as a place can be while still having sand. I have spent time in both, and eight weeks studying in London on top of that, and the useful part of going somewhere unfamiliar is not the view. It is that you stop treating the way things work at home as the way things work.

London was the first time I saw a services market that had already been consolidated. The trades, the facilities businesses, the contractors, categories that in Florida are still thousands of family-owned shops, had a handful of real players, and everyone in the industry knew their names.

That gap is not a curiosity. It is the entire lower-middle-market thesis stated as a geography. At Waterbrook I screened more than 120 targets between $5M and $250M of enterprise value across fragmented industrials and services verticals, and the reason that list ran so long is that the American version of those markets has not happened yet.

I would rather help build the consolidated version of those markets than study it after somebody else has.

Three interests, one habit: I am drawn to the part of a thing that does not show up in the result. The save, the position, the market that has not consolidated yet. None of that is a reason to want the sell-side, where the work ends at close. All of it is a reason to want to own the outcome.

Project Alpha

Palmetto Partners — FSU Annual Mock Competition

A buy-side case on a mechanical, electrical, and plumbing contractor in Greenwood, Mississippi, generating roughly $168M of revenue across four divisions serving hospitals, schools, and industrial clients throughout the Sunbelt. We built it on three pillars:

  • An M&A platform in a market fragmented enough to actually consolidate
  • Margin expansion through BIM in estimating and centralized back-office functions
  • Sunbelt construction growth, data center demand, and aging infrastructure as tailwind

We held entry and exit at 7.5x EBITDA, so the returns had to come from operations and deleveraging rather than from multiple expansion.

What I’d bring to Recalc

Community, Mentorship, and What I’m Missing

I was a mentee in FSU’s Investment Banking & Alternatives Academy before I was a mentor in it, and that order is the whole point. I am now one of a class of 16 in Noles on Wall Street, one of sixty in Access Distributed, and a fellow in the Roark Capital Wall Street Club program. Every one of those rooms exists because students at Florida State built them. None of them came with the school.

That is the honest version of my position. I have not lacked for effort or for programs. What I have not had is someone who actually ran deals sitting next to me while I build a model and telling me where my logic breaks.

Why Recalc specifically

  1. Modeling taught by people who used it

    Katie Fifer spent a decade in private equity at Audax Group and Paxion Capital, and Suzanne Corley came through J.P. Morgan. The LBO block is not a recorded course, it is seven live Tuesday sessions where someone who has underwritten real platforms can see the assumption I got wrong while I am still typing it.

  2. Accounting as the foundation, not the afterthought

    I chose Finance and Accounting as a double major for the same reason the curriculum opens on accounting fundamentals before it touches an LBO. Most students want the model. The model is downstream of knowing what the statements are actually telling you.

  3. A partner list that includes credit

    TCW Private Credit sits alongside Audax, Charlesbank, Integrum, Silversmith, Spectrum Equity, and TSG Consumer among the firms backing the program. Two of my four seats so far have been credit seats. That overlap is the most specific reason I am applying here rather than anywhere else.

  4. The Slack is a commitment, not a perk

    Consistent engagement and professional follow-through on introductions are stated requirements, not bonuses. I would rather be held to that than left alone with a recording.

Draft — the one block I will not write for you

You asked me to invent what your friend in the program told you. I have not, and I won’t. Recalc scores authentic communication, the reader may well know the person, and a fabricated quote is the one failure here that cannot be walked back.

What I’d bring

  • A credit lens most sophomores do not have. Spreads, coverage, and structure, from two seats, before ever building an LBO.
  • Sourcing reps, not only modeling reps. 120+ lower-middle-market targets screened and mapped to decision-makers.
  • The habit of answering last year’s question. Mentee first, mentor second, in that order, and I intend to keep that order in your Slack.

I am applying to Recalc because the gap between my résumé and my ability is a modeling gap, and you teach exactly that, taught by people who have done it. Give me the seat and I will be the member who shows up to all seven, answers in the Slack, and comes back next year to do it for somebody else.
